Keep tables and images when converting PDF to Word

Practical checks for preserving tables, images, captions and document structure after PDF-to-Word conversion.

Tables and images are often the hardest parts of a PDF to reconstruct as editable Word content. A PDF stores the visual page, while Word needs editable objects and document structure.

PDFOrbit limitation: The local converter extracts selectable text. It does not currently reconstruct table cells or copy embedded images into DOCX.

Why tables can move

Some PDFs contain real table structure, while others only place text at fixed coordinates. A converter may need to infer rows, columns and cell boundaries from the page.

How to review tables after conversion

  • Check merged cells and column widths.
  • Look for text that moved outside a cell.
  • Review borders, alignment and row spacing.
  • Make sure page breaks do not split important rows awkwardly.

How images are handled

Images usually convert more reliably than complex text layouts, but captions, wrapping and exact positioning can change. In Word, review image size, text wrapping and anchor position.

Tip: For important business documents, always compare the DOCX beside the original PDF before sharing the converted file.
